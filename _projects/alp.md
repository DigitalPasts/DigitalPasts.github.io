Ancient Language Processing (ALP) is the subfield of natural language processing devoted to ancient languages and scripts, from the first writing in Mesopotamia and Egypt around 3000 BCE to late antiquity. It brings together philologists, historians, epigraphers and computer scientists around problems that modern NLP rarely meets: very small corpora, broken texts, scripts that standard tokenizers cannot handle, non-alphabetic and right-to-left writing, and transliteration conventions that differ from field to field.

## Building the field

- **Workshops.** The lab co-founded the ALP workshop series at the main NLP venues: ALP 2023 at RANLP (Varna), the Ancient Language Translation workshop at MT Summit 2023 (Macau), ML4AL 2024 at ACL (Bangkok) and ALP 2025 at NAACL. A third ALP workshop is proposed for 2027. Each edition publishes its proceedings in the ACL Anthology.
- **Shared tasks.** EvaCun sets common benchmarks for cuneiform languages: machine translation in 2023, lemmatization and token prediction with large language models in 2025, and a new edition with ALP 2027.
- **Teaching.** An international graduate course in Ancient Language Processing, taught with Prof. Eliese-Sophia Lincke (FU Berlin), and the Ancient Language Processing colloquium of the DANES network.

## Our research

The lab's work began with Akkadian cuneiform and now spans many ancient languages: machine translation from Akkadian and Sumerian to English, lemmatization and language models for Hittite, an Elamite linked open data dictionary, classification of proto-cuneiform accounts, and annotation workflows for Semitic inscriptions. Much of it feeds back into tools such as the [Babylonian Engine]({{ '/research/ben/' | relative_url }}).
